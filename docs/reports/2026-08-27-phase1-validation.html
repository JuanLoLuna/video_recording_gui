Video Recorder Phase 1

Hardening the SmartSleeve video-recording GUI to survive a 10-day unattended session: sleep prevention, unbounded audio, crash-safe metadata, and camera-fault recovery — validated on the real acquisition rig, including a genuine 17-second camera dropout.

Summary

The recording GUI was built and tuned for sessions of a few minutes to half an hour — real ADL sessions in the study data run 2–32 minutes. A 10-day continuous session breaks assumptions that duration never surfaced: metadata buffered entirely in RAM, a WAV format with a hard 4 GiB ceiling, an acquisition loop with no recovery from a camera fault, and nothing stopping Windows from sleeping mid-session.

Four fixes shipped and merged into main this period, backed by 98 unit tests and, for the fault-recovery path, a real camera unplug/replug on the acquisition rig — not just an emulated failure.

What broke, and how it’s handled

One finding this period, caught by the user’s own testing rather than by inspection.

01The recording-integrity warning stayed red for the rest of a session after the watchdog fixed the fault
Symptom
During the camera unplug/replug test, the GUI’s integrity-warning text turned red as expected during the outage — but stayed red for the remainder of the recording even after the watchdog recovered cleanly, with no way to tell "still broken" from "fixed a while ago."
Cause
→ _recording_integrity_warning was a plain boolean, set once on the first fault and only ever cleared at the start of the next recording — a latch, not a health indicator.
Found by
The user’s own hardware test, not a code review — the exact kind of thing that only shows up once you actually recover from a real fault and keep watching the screen afterward.
Result
Fixed — RecordingWarningTracker now tracks active/recovered/hidden state from event timestamps instead of a latch, and the banner can be dismissed once read, reopening only on a genuine new episode.

The general lesson: a health indicator driven by a boolean that only ever goes one direction isn’t a health indicator, it’s an incident log with one entry. Anything meant to answer "is it okay right now" needs a window and a recovery path built in from the start, not bolted on after the first time someone notices it stuck.

Open items

Overnight / multi-hour soak test

Confirms sleep prevention actually holds for hours (not just the short test window used so far) and that the WAV genuinely grows past the old ~13.5h plain-WAV ceiling. Not yet run.

Kill-process mid-recording test

Confirms the metadata CSV survives up to within a few seconds of a hard kill, rather than being empty or truncated to nothing. Not yet run.

Phase 3 backlog

A ranked list of remaining correctness/observability items — the power auto-stop still shows a blocking modal instead of pausing gracefully, the teardown path doesn’t yet wait for an in-flight camera reinit, audio xruns aren’t counted and a dropped mic doesn’t reconnect. Not started.

Windows power-plan settings

SetThreadExecutionState only suppresses idle sleep — it’s ignored on Modern Standby, lid-close, and user-initiated sleep. The powercfg standby/hibernate/disk timeouts and lid-close=Do Nothing settings on the actual recording machine are a required operational backstop, unconfirmed as of this report.

05

Where this leaves things

The four survival fixes are shipped, merged, and validated against a real fault on real hardware — a 17-second camera dropout recovered with zero metadata loss and zero frame-index disruption, which is exactly the failure mode a 10-day unattended session was expected to hit. The soak and kill-process tests are what stand between "validated against the failures we deliberately caused" and "confirmed for the actual duration and shutdown modes the study needs." Phase 2 (video segmentation, covered in its own report) builds directly on top of this, including reusing the same watchdog to force a segment boundary on a fault.
